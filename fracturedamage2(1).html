Variational Models of Fracture and Damage

My research develops mathematically rigorous models for fracture and damage by combining the calculus of variations, shape and topology optimization, asymptotic analysis and finite-element approximation. The objective is to predict crack initiation and propagation without prescribing the crack path a priori, while preserving a rigorous variational framework.

Shape Optimization and Fracture Evolution

My first contributions to fracture mechanics, developed in collaboration with Grégoire Allaire and François Jouve, formulated crack evolution as a shape-optimization problem. Rather than prescribing the geometry of the crack, the damaged region is treated as an evolving geometric object governed by the mechanical energy.

Shape derivatives identify energetically favourable boundary motions and provide a variational criterion for crack propagation. This establishes a direct connection between fracture mechanics, optimal-shape theory and the calculus of variations.

Topological Derivatives and Crack Nucleation

While shape derivatives describe the evolution of existing cracks, topological derivatives identify the energetically favourable locations at which new defects should nucleate. They measure the leading-order variation of the elastic energy produced by introducing an infinitesimal crack.

This approach avoids prescribing crack nucleation sites in advance. It couples asymptotic sensitivity analysis with numerical fracture simulation and provides a unified framework in which nucleation and propagation are driven by energy variations.

Level-Set Methods for Crack Propagation

Once nucleated, cracks are represented implicitly by level-set functions. This representation allows the crack geometry to evolve naturally under the stress field without remeshing the computational domain.

The level-set method can describe complex crack paths, branching and interaction while retaining a fixed finite-element mesh. The evolving geometry is therefore separated from the underlying discretization, which improves robustness and flexibility.

Hydraulic Fracture

Hydraulic fracture couples fracture mechanics with fluid pressure and transport. Together with collaborators, I developed formulations governed by topological derivatives in which hydro-mechanical effects enter directly into the fracture indicator.

These models combine asymptotic sensitivity analysis, finite-element approximation and level-set tracking to predict crack nucleation and propagation in porous and pressurized media.

Damage and Coupled Models

Another line of research concerns continuum damage mechanics and ductile fracture. In collaboration with Paulo Areias and engineering colleagues, I developed models involving finite strains, temperature-dependent effects and configurational forces.

These formulations aim to describe crack initiation and propagation under complex loading conditions while retaining a consistent energetic and variational structure.

Numerical Approximation

Numerical approximation combines finite-element discretizations, level-set methods and shape and topological sensitivity analysis. The resulting algorithms are designed to predict crack nucleation, propagation and interaction while remaining consistent with the underlying variational formulation.

Particular emphasis is placed on benchmark computations, numerical robustness and comparison with classical fracture formulations.
