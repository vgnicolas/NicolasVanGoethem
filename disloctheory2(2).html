Research Area

Defects, Dislocations and Topological Singularities

This research programme combines continuum mechanics, geometric measure theory, partial differential equations and the calculus of variations to describe crystalline defects across several length scales.

A Distributional Approach to Dislocations

Classical smooth displacement fields cannot globally represent a crystal containing dislocations. The distributional formulation avoids artificial branch cuts and describes Burgers vectors and dislocation densities through intrinsic singular measures and differential operators.

This perspective provides a bridge between isolated defects, intrinsic elasticity, the incompatibility operator and continuum theories with distributed dislocation densities.

Currents, Dislocation Lines and Variational Evolution

In three dimensions, dislocations are curved oriented line defects. Cartesian currents retain orientation, multiplicity and boundary information while allowing weak convergence and singular limits.

With R. Scala, I developed variational and quasistatic evolution models based on incremental minimization, balancing stored elastic energy with the cost of moving dislocations while preserving their geometric structure and conservation laws.

Jacobians and Topological Singularities

With L. De Luca and R. Scala, I introduced a weak notion of Jacobian for BV maps. It captures topological singularities in discontinuous fields through distributional derivatives, currents and geometric measure theory.

Geometry, Incompatibility and Defective Crystals

Dislocations are associated with torsion, while strain incompatibility is related to linearized curvature. This geometric viewpoint leads to intrinsic formulations of elasticity, the Frank tensor, Kröner’s formula and compatible–incompatible decompositions.

From Discrete Defects to Continuum Plasticity

A long-term objective is to derive effective continuum theories from large systems of interacting dislocations by combining compactness, relaxation, Γ-convergence and homogenization with the geometric constraints carried by dislocation currents.
