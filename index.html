About

I am Associate Professor at the Department of Mathematics of the Faculty of Sciences, University of Lisbon, and a researcher at CEMS.UL.

My research focuses on the mathematical modelling, analysis, and numerical simulation of materials and structures. I work at the intersection of continuum mechanics, applied analysis, and materials science, with interests spanning elasticity, elasto-plasticity, fracture, damage, and dislocation theory. My approach combines variational methods, partial differential equations, geometric measure theory, differential geometry, and topological sensitivity analysis to develop rigorous mathematical frameworks and computational tools for the study of solids with defects and evolving microstructures.

Research Highlights

  • Mathematical theory of dislocations and incompatibility in crystalline solids.
  • Variational modelling of fracture, damage and crack propagation.
  • Intrinsic formulations of elasticity and elasto-plasticity governed by incompatibility operators.
  • Topological sensitivity analysis and shape optimization methods for fracture mechanics.
  • Fields of bounded deformation (BD), relaxation and homogenization.
  • Numerical simulation of complex mechanical systems and evolving microstructures.

Research Interests

Continuum Mechanics

Elasticity, elasto-plasticity, constitutive modelling and thermodynamics.

Dislocation Theory

Geometric and analytical descriptions of defects in crystalline solids.

Fracture & Damage

Variational and computational approaches to crack initiation and propagation.

Calculus of Variations

Γ-convergence, relaxation, homogenization and free-discontinuity problems.

Topological Sensitivity

Shape and topology optimization applied to fracture and damage mechanics.

Geometric Analysis & PDEs

Bounded deformation, incompatibility operators, geometric measure theory and nonlinear PDEs.
