Research Area

Fracture and Damage Mechanics

My research develops variational and computational methods for crack initiation, propagation and material degradation, without prescribing the crack path in advance.

Shape Optimization and Crack Evolution

During my postdoctoral work with G. Allaire and F. Jouve, we developed a shape-optimization approach in which cracks are evolving geometric objects driven by variations of the mechanical energy.

Shape derivatives identify energetically favourable boundary motions. The crack geometry is tracked by level-set methods, avoiding remeshing and allowing the damaged region to adapt to the stress field.

Topological Derivatives and Crack Nucleation

The topological derivative measures the leading-order energy variation produced by inserting an infinitesimal defect. It provides a spatial indicator for the loci and orientations at which crack nucleation is energetically favourable.

The same sensitivity information can drive both nucleation and propagation, yielding flexible computational models for brittle fracture in solids and plates.

Hydro-Mechanical Fracture

In hydraulic fracture, crack evolution is coupled with fluid pressure and transport. Our topological-derivative formulation incorporates this coupling directly into the fracture indicator and connects asymptotic analysis with numerical simulation.

Damage, Ductile Fracture and Coupled Models

With P. Areias and engineering collaborators, I have also worked on finite-strain, temperature-dependent and configurational-force formulations for ductile fracture, as well as coupled damage models involving additional physical fields.

Variational Structure and Numerical Approximation

The programme combines variational analysis, asymptotic sensitivity methods, finite-element computation and level-set tracking. Its objective is both to predict crack paths and to identify the mathematical structures that make these predictions stable and physically meaningful.
